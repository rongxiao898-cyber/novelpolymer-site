MEDICAL & LIGHT-DELIVERY FIBER GUIDE

How to Specify Low-Index Coatings for Medical and Laser-Delivery Fiber

A low-index coating for medical or laser-delivery fiber should be specified from the optical architecture and intended use conditions. The application label does not establish medical-device certification or a power-handling guarantee.

Send your application briefView all technical guides
01

Define the optical architecture

Document the glass composition or structure, core and cladding geometry, coating location, target dimensions and the role the low-index layer performs in the finished fiber.

02

Specify refractive index with measurement conditions

State wavelength, temperature, specimen preparation, test method and whether the value refers to liquid or cured material. Values obtained under different conditions are not directly interchangeable.

03

Map the coating to the transmission requirement

Define the intended wavelength range, numerical-aperture or cladding-light requirement and relevant bend conditions. Do not infer finished-fiber transmission or power capability from refractive index alone.

04

Include manufacturing and handling conditions

Record coating application, cure setup, dimensions, winding and handling, connectorization, cleaning and any application-specific environmental or sterilization sequence that the finished fiber must withstand.

05

Qualify the finished fiber and device path

Measure the optical, mechanical and environmental behavior of fiber made with the intended materials and process. Device-level safety, compliance and lifetime require their own qualification.

INFORMATION CHECKLIST

Prepare these details for a productive material discussion.

  • Fiber architecture and coating location
  • Wavelength and refractive-index measurement conditions
  • Transmission, NA or cladding-light requirement
  • Coating, cure and handling process
  • Finished-fiber and device-level qualification plan

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Does the medical-fiber application label establish medical-device certification?

No. It identifies an intended fiber application only. Biocompatibility, sterilization compatibility, device safety and regulatory compliance require separate evidence.

Can refractive index alone establish laser-delivery performance?

No. Fiber geometry, wavelength, coating dimensions, bend conditions, power exposure, process quality and finished-fiber tests also affect performance.

Which conditions must accompany a refractive-index value?

At minimum, identify wavelength, temperature, material state, specimen preparation and test method.
