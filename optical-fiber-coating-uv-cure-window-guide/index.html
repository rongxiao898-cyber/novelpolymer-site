PROCESS VALIDATION GUIDE

How to Define and Validate a UV-Cure Window for Optical Fiber Coatings

A useful UV-cure window connects the actual coating line and fiber geometry to measurable cure and finished-fiber acceptance criteria; one nominal lamp setting is not enough.

Discuss a coating cure-window evaluationView all technical guides
01

Define the coating line and geometry

Record line speed, lamp positions, distance to the coated fiber, coating dimensions and the number and arrangement of UV sources. These conditions determine the exposure geometry that must be reproduced.

02

Characterize the UV source at the process location

Document the source type, relevant wavelength range, measured irradiance or dose method, instrument and calibration status. A machine setting should not be treated as a measured value.

03

Include material and environmental variables

Control coating temperature, oxygen exposure or inerting conditions, material age and any other variable that can change surface or through-cure behavior.

04

Build the window around controlled trials

Vary the relevant line and UV settings within safe equipment limits, record the actual measured conditions and inspect the primary and outer layers separately where the process uses both. Surface condition alone does not establish the degree of cure.

05

Set finished-fiber acceptance criteria

Define coating integrity, dimensions, adhesion or surface checks and the optical, mechanical and environmental tests required for fiber produced inside the proposed process window.

INFORMATION CHECKLIST

Prepare these details for a productive material discussion.

  • Coating system and target dimensions
  • Line speed and UV-source geometry
  • Measured irradiance or dose method
  • Atmosphere, temperature and material condition
  • Cure indicators and finished-fiber acceptance tests

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Does one recommended UV dose define the complete process window?

No. Source spectrum, geometry, line speed, coating dimensions, atmosphere and measurement method all affect the exposure received by the coating.

Can a lamp power setting be compared directly between coating lines?

Not reliably. Measure and document the UV conditions at the relevant process location using an agreed method.

Why should finished fiber be tested after cure trials?

The practical decision concerns coated-fiber performance, so material or surface checks should be connected to optical, mechanical and environmental acceptance tests on the finished fiber.
