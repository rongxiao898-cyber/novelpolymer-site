TECHNICAL GUIDE

How to Select an Optical Fiber Coating System

Coating selection works best when the fiber architecture, the function of each layer and the complete draw-and-cure process are evaluated together.

Discuss your applicationView all technical guides
01

Start with the fiber architecture

Define the fiber type, glass geometry, target coating dimensions and the mechanical or optical sensitivities that matter in the finished construction.

02

Separate the roles of the coating layers

In a dual-layer system, the primary layer and protective outer layer perform different functions. Evaluate their balance as a system rather than comparing either layer in isolation.

03

Map the material to the draw process

Record the coating application method, line speed, available cure energy, thermal history and dimensional controls. A material data sheet does not replace process-window evaluation.

04

Compare data under matching conditions

Refractive index, viscosity, modulus and other values should be compared only when wavelength, temperature, material state and test method are consistent.

05

Validate on the finished fiber

Use application-relevant optical, mechanical, environmental and handling tests on fiber made with the intended geometry and process.

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Can a coating be selected from one numerical property?

No. The layer function, fiber geometry, process window and finished-fiber tests should be evaluated together.

Why should primary and outer coatings be considered as a system?

The two layers have different roles and their interaction affects processing, protection and finished-fiber performance.

What information should be shared with a coating supplier?

Share the fiber structure, coating dimensions, line and cure conditions, operating environment and required qualification tests.
