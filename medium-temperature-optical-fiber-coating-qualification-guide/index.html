MEDIUM-TEMPERATURE FIBER GUIDE

How to Qualify Optical Fiber Coatings for 150–200°C Environments

A target temperature range does not by itself establish continuous service life. Qualification must define exposure duration, atmosphere, thermal cycling and the optical and mechanical properties retained by the finished coated fiber.

Send your application briefView all technical guides
01

Define the thermal duty cycle

State the target temperature range, continuous or intermittent exposure, dwell time, heating and cooling rates, number of cycles and any expected excursions beyond the normal condition.

02

Document atmosphere and adjacent materials

Record air, inert gas, vacuum, humidity, chemicals and cable or packaging materials that can influence oxidation, moisture uptake, adhesion or stress during aging.

03

Establish the coated-fiber baseline

Document the glass, primary and outer coating materials, dimensions, draw and cure process, conditioning and baseline optical and mechanical measurements before exposure.

04

Measure retention after defined exposures

Check coating appearance and integrity together with application-relevant attenuation, adhesion, strip behavior, tensile or handling tests. Use consistent conditioning and measurement methods.

05

Compare material routes without merging ratings

A dual-layer UV-curable system and a polyimide route use different materials and processes. Compare them against the same application requirements without substituting Tg, decomposition temperature or short-term exposure for service life.

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Does a 150–200°C target range establish continuous-use life?

No. Service life depends on duration, cycling, atmosphere, fiber construction, process and the acceptance criteria demonstrated after exposure.

Can Tg or decomposition temperature be used as a service-temperature rating?

No. They are material-analysis results and do not replace finished-fiber aging and retention data.

Why should both coating layers be evaluated together?

The primary layer, outer layer, interface, dimensions and shared cure process form one finished coating system.
