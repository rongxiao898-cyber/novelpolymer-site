THERMAL DATA GUIDE

How to Interpret Thermal Data for Polyimide Fiber Coatings

Thermal data must be interpreted by test method and material state before it is used to define a coated fiber application window.

Discuss your applicationView all technical guides
01

Identify what the number represents

A glass-transition temperature, decomposition temperature, cure temperature and application operating temperature describe different events and should not be substituted for one another.

02

Check the specimen and test method

Confirm whether the result comes from a cured film, liquid formulation or coated fiber, and record the analytical method, atmosphere and heating profile.

03

Separate process temperature from service temperature

A coating may experience elevated temperature during cure or manufacture without that temperature being a continuous-use rating.

04

Include time and environment

Thermal exposure duration, cycling, humidity, chemicals, pressure or vacuum and mechanical load can change the relevant qualification requirement.

05

Validate the finished coated fiber

Establish application limits through optical, dimensional and mechanical testing on the intended fiber construction after the required exposure sequence.

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Is decomposition temperature a continuous operating temperature?

No. It is an analytical result under defined test conditions and does not establish a continuous-use rating.

Is cure temperature the same as service temperature?

No. Cure is a manufacturing step; service capability requires separate application-specific validation.

What should a thermal qualification plan include?

Include the fiber construction, exposure temperature and duration, environment, number of cycles and optical and mechanical retention criteria.
