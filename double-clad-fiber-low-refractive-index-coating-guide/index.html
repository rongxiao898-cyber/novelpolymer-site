DOUBLE-CLAD FIBER GUIDE

How to Specify Low-Index Coatings for Double-Clad Fiber

A useful low-index coating specification connects the refractive-index requirement to the fiber architecture, measurement conditions, coating process and finished-fiber validation plan.

Send your application briefView all technical guides
01

Map the coating to the fiber architecture

Document the glass structure, the position of the low-index coating, target coating dimensions and the optical role that the coating must support in the finished fiber.

02

Specify refractive index under defined conditions

State the measurement wavelength, temperature, specimen preparation and test method. Values measured under different conditions should not be treated as directly interchangeable.

03

Separate liquid and cured refractive-index values

Identify whether each value refers to the uncured liquid or cured material. A liquid measurement should not be used as a substitute for a cured-coating result.

04

Define the coating and cure process

Record viscosity requirements, application method, line conditions, cure access or energy, coating dimensions and any process limits that could affect the finished layer.

05

Validate the finished fiber

Evaluate optical performance, coating integrity and relevant mechanical and environmental behavior on fiber produced with the intended geometry and process.

INFORMATION CHECKLIST

Prepare these details for a productive material discussion.

  • Fiber structure and low-index coating location
  • Target refractive index and measurement conditions
  • Liquid or cured material state
  • Coating dimensions and process setup
  • Finished-fiber acceptance tests

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Can liquid and cured refractive-index values be compared as the same property?

No. They represent different material states and should be specified and compared separately.

Is refractive index alone enough to choose a low-index coating?

No. The fiber architecture, coating dimensions, drawing and cure process, mechanical requirements and finished-fiber tests must also be considered.

What information should be provided for a coating evaluation?

Provide the fiber structure, coating location and dimensions, target refractive index with measurement conditions, process setup and finished-fiber acceptance tests.
