OPTICAL MODULE ASSEMBLY GUIDE

How to Select Adhesives for Optical Module Assembly

An optical module can contain several bond functions, so adhesive selection should begin with the exact location, substrates, optical sensitivity and assembly process rather than one universal material specification.

Send your application briefView all technical guides
01

Map every adhesive location and function

Separate optical alignment fixation, structural support, fiber strain relief, potting, sealing and temporary process fixation. Each location can require a different balance of flow, rigidity, flexibility and removal behavior.

02

Document substrates and the optical path

Identify glass, ceramic, metal, semiconductor or polymer surfaces, surface preparation, coatings and any region where adhesive, outgassing or contamination could affect an optical interface.

03

Define dispensing and bond-line geometry

Record gap, fillet, dispense volume, capillary path, placement tolerance and access for inspection. A bulk material value does not predict behavior in an uncontrolled bond geometry.

04

Control fixation and complete cure

Document UV access, measured exposure conditions, thermal steps, shadowed regions, fixture strategy and the sequence used to reach complete cure. Measure alignment before, during and after the intended cure process where relevant.

05

Qualify the finished assembly

Evaluate optical alignment or insertion loss together with adhesion, thermal cycling, damp heat, mechanical loading and any application-specific environment using defined baselines, controls and acceptance limits.

INFORMATION CHECKLIST

Prepare these details for a productive material discussion.

  • Bond locations and functions
  • Substrates, surfaces and optical path
  • Dispense and bond-line geometry
  • Fixation, UV access and complete cure
  • Optical, mechanical and environmental qualification

TECHNICAL FAQ

Questions to clarify before evaluation

Should one adhesive be used for every bond in an optical module?

Not automatically. Alignment fixation, structural support, strain relief, potting and temporary process steps can impose different requirements.

Is optical transparency required for every module adhesive?

No. It is relevant only when the adhesive is intentionally in or near an optical path; opaque structural or potting locations should be specified by their actual function.

Is low cure shrinkage enough to predict alignment stability?

No. Bond geometry, substrates, fixture constraints, cure sequence, dispense volume and thermal or moisture exposure also influence assembly movement.
